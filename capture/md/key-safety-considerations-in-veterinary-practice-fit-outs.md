# Key Safety Considerations in Veterinary Practice Fit-Outs

 March 18, 2026

![](https://timdayconstruction.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/DSCF5244-scaled.jpg)

Designing and delivering a veterinary practice fit-out involves far more than creating functional treatment rooms and waiting areas. Veterinary clinics must support high standards of hygiene, ensure the safety of staff and animals, and comply with a range of regulatory requirements.

From radiation protection to infection control, thoughtful planning and specialist materials are essential to create environments that are safe, practical, and compliant. Understanding these considerations early in the design process can help ensure smoother project delivery and long-term operational efficiency.

At Tim Day Construction, we bring experience in delivering specialist veterinary fit-outs and understand the unique safety, compliance, and operational requirements involved in these projects.

**Radiation Shielding in X-Ray Rooms**

Many veterinary practices include diagnostic imaging facilities, which means radiation protection must be carefully considered during the fit-out stage.

Materials such as lead-lined plasterboard or products like Knauf Safeboard are commonly used within X-ray rooms to provide effective radiation shielding. These materials help contain radiation within the imaging area and protect staff and other occupants of the building.

At Tim Day Construction, we’ve used Knauf Safeboard to ensure full radiation protection in their imaging room. The Safeboard and specialist joint filler contains barium sulphate to create a radiation-shielding barrier, eliminating the need for traditional lead lining.

Proper shielding design should also consider wall construction, door systems and equipment placement. With the door closed, visibility into imaging rooms are fully blocked for safety, so video link systems are commonly installed to allow the vets to monitor procedures securely.

Early coordination between designers, equipment suppliers, and radiation protection specialists ensures compliance with safety requirements while maintaining practical working layouts.

**Infection Control and Hygienic Surfaces**

Veterinary clinics manage a wide range of animals, many of which may carry infectious diseases. As a result, infection control is a critical design consideration.

Surfaces throughout treatment and clinical areas should be:

* Non-porous and easy to clean
* Resistant to disinfectants and chemicals
* Durable enough to withstand regular washing and sanitation

Common materials include seamless vinyl flooring, antimicrobial wall finishes, and stainless steel surfaces in treatment areas. Minimising joints, seams, and difficult-to-clean corners helps reduce the risk of contamination and supports efficient cleaning routines.

**Ventilation and Odour Control**

Ventilation plays an important role in maintaining air quality within veterinary facilities. Clinics must manage a combination of odours, airborne contaminants, and potential pathogens.

Well-designed ventilation systems can help:

* Maintain fresh airflow throughout the building
* Reduce the spread of airborne pathogens
* Control odours from treatment rooms, kennels, and surgical areas

In some areas, such as isolation wards or operating theatres, specialist ventilation strategies may be required to maintain appropriate environmental conditions.

**Safe Animal Handling Layouts**

Unlike many healthcare environments, veterinary practices must be designed to accommodate both people and animals. Layout planning should prioritise safe movement and minimise stress for animals.

Practical considerations include:

* Clear separation between waiting, treatment, and recovery areas
* Adequate circulation space for staff handling animals
* Safe access routes to diagnostic and surgical rooms
* Minimising opportunities for animals to encounter one another unexpectedly

Thoughtful layouts not only improve safety but also help reduce anxiety for both animals and their owners.

**Flooring and Slip Resistance**

Veterinary environments often experience frequent spills, cleaning, and heavy foot traffic. Flooring must therefore be durable, hygienic, and safe for both animals and staff.

Slip-resistant flooring is particularly important to prevent injuries. Materials such as safety vinyl or resin flooring systems provide grip while remaining easy to clean and maintain.

In addition, flooring should be robust enough to withstand scratching from claws, frequent cleaning chemicals, and the movement of equipment.

**Waste Disposal and Biohazard Storage**

Veterinary practices generate a range of clinical and biological waste, including sharps, contaminated materials, and pharmaceutical waste.

Safe waste management systems must be incorporated into the design of the facility, including:

* Dedicated clinical waste storage areas
* Secure sharps disposal systems
* Clearly defined waste handling routes
* Compliance with relevant waste management regulations

Proper storage and disposal processes help protect staff, maintain hygiene standards, and ensure regulatory compliance.

**Designing for Safety and Efficiency**

A successful veterinary practice fit-out balances functionality, compliance, and safety. By addressing key considerations such as radiation protection, infection control, ventilation, and safe layouts early in the design process, architects and practice owners can create facilities that support both clinical excellence and operational efficiency.

Careful planning not only protects staff and animals but also ensures that veterinary practices remain safe, compliant, and fit for purpose for years to come.
