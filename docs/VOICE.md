How Tim Day Construction sounds, so new pages, project write-ups, FAQs and posts read like the existing site and like Tim. This was learned from all 20,400 words of live copy (13 posts, 13 pages, 14 projects, 5 sectors), captured 23 October 2026. The quotes below come from the live site.

## 1. Who we're talking to

Proof: Vet Shack (Canterbury) and Pure Vet (Ashford) |

The live site also promotes extensions, remodelling, refurbishment, change of use and listed buildings. Those pages stay in v1 as they are. New residential copy should lead with **new builds**.

## 2. Voice traits

**Tim, speaking for his team.** Tim's own words are the benchmark: "I want my clients to ENJOY the build not ENDURE the build… raising a glass of bubbly with me and the team at the end of the job."

| Trait | What it means | Sounds like (live copy) | Not like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Say the thing plainly, then back it up | "Timescales and costs are dependent on many factors, but one thing that's non-negotiable for us is quality." | "We strive to deliver excellence across all touchpoints." |
| **Warm** | People first: clients, the team, the animals in the practice | "They are also really nice people to work with" (client). "a comfortable environment for both the veterinary team and their animal patients" | Cold spec-sheet copy with no people in it |
| **No-nonsense** | Practical, specific and honest about what's hard | "There's no point in choosing an architect who's good at heritage projects but you want a modern beach side new build." | Hype, superlatives, vague promises |
| **Confident** | Proud of the work without boasting | "big enough to manage £1m+ contracts, but small enough to care about the details" | "Kent's number one award-winning leading builder" |
| **Local** | Rooted in Canterbury and Kent, and names real places | "Based just outside Canterbury, we work across Kent, including Whitstable, Faversham, Herne Bay…" | Generic "nationwide" language |

The register is spoken, not corporate. Contractions are fine and common ("we're", "you'll", "that's"). Exclamation marks are used sparingly: about one per page, usually at the end of a warm line ("…recommend to anyone who'll listen!").

## 3. Core messages

Use these consistently. Don't invent new taglines.

1. **"Concept to completion."** One team from the earliest design conversations to handover. This is the home H1 and a recurring phrase. Write it as *concept to completion* in body copy and *'Concept to completion'* (curly single quotes) when quoted as the tagline.
2. **"Enjoy the build, not endure it."** The client-experience promise, in Tim's words. Variants on the live site: "allow them to enjoy the process, rather than endure it", "Our mission is for you to 'Enjoy the build, not endure it!'"
3. **"Builders who build trust."** For example: "We're builders who build trust, along with exceptional spaces."
4. **Get us involved early.** Early engagement finds cost savings and avoids redesigns: "We're builders who add value from day one."
5. **Transparent communication.** "We don't hide anything. We share everything with you promptly."
6. **Big enough, small enough.** £1m+ contracts, personal attention.
7. **Specialist in veterinary and clinical fit-outs.** Compliance-led and designed around the people (and animals) who use the space.
8. **Proven and accredited.** FMB, NHBC, Passive House Tradesperson, SMAS Worksafe. Over 20 years running TDC and around 25 years building.

## 4. Style rules

### Spelling and grammar
- **UK English:** -ise (specialise, minimise, organise), colour, programme (for a construction schedule), whilst (acceptable, used on the site), licence (noun).
- **Company name:** "Tim Day Construction" on first mention in any page or section. After that use "we/our" or "TDC" (sparingly; it's fine in project write-ups). Never "Tim Day Construction Ltd" except in the footer's legal line.
- **Tim:** "Tim" or "Tim Day", never "Mr Day".
- **Person:** "we" for TDC, "you" for the reader. Tim's founder copy is first person singular ("I'm Tim Day…").
- **Place names:** Canterbury, Whitstable, Herne Bay, Faversham, Ashford, Thanet, Elham, Kent, "the South East" (no hyphen, capital S and E).

### Punctuation
- **No em dashes.** The live site uses none. For a parenthetical use commas or brackets, and use a full stop for a break.
- **En dash** only in number ranges, unspaced ("6–12 months"). The spaced en-dash list on the home page ("Saving time – Reducing costs – …") is legacy; don't copy it.
- **Curly quotes and apostrophes** throughout (' ' " "). Straight quotes are a bug.
- **Oxford comma:** the site is split roughly 50/50. **Rule for new copy: no Oxford comma** (UK default), unless leaving it out causes ambiguity.
- **Ampersand** only in names and short headings ("Design & Build", "Clinical & Veterinary", "Major Extensions & Refurbishments"). Use "and" in sentences.
- **Numbers:** numerals for 10 and over, and for all measurements, money and durations ("6–12 months", "£1m+", "20 years"). Use words for one to nine in running prose unless technical.
- **Phone:** 01227 281 749. **Email:** info@timdayconstruction.co.uk.

### Capitalisation
- **Title Case** for page titles, H1s, post titles, project names and section headings (H2): "Veterinary Fit-Out Specialists", "Where We Work".
- **Sentence case** for FAQ questions, H3 lead lines and card blurbs: "When should I appoint a contractor for a veterinary fit-out?"
- **Hyphenation:** "fit-out" (noun and adjective), "Fit-Outs" in Title Case. Never "fitout" or "fit out" as the noun. Also "new build" (noun), "new-build standards" (adjective), "design and build" in prose, "Design & Build" as the service name.
- **Lower case** for veterinary, practice and architect unless starting a heading. The live site sometimes writes "Veterinary practices" and "Architects" mid-sentence; that's legacy.

## 6. Words to use and avoid

**Use (they're TDC's words):** concept to completion · enjoy the build · trusted · committed · transparent · early engagement · hands-on · attention to detail · practical · compliant · fit-out · new build · bespoke (for homes) · craftsmanship · on budget, on schedule · the team · clients · local · Kent and named towns.

**Avoid:**

| Avoid | Why / use instead |
|---|---|
| Em dashes (—) | Not in the house style; use commas or a full stop |
| Keyword stuffing ("builders in Kent" again and again) | One legacy post ("Common Challenges on Kent Building Sites") repeats it 7 times. New copy names Kent or a town naturally, **once or twice per page** |
| "Solutions" on its own ("construction solutions") | Say what the thing is: "a new roof with solar panels" |
| Superlatives with no proof ("leading", "best", "number one", "world-class") | Give the evidence: years, accreditations, a named project |
| Corporate filler ("leverage", "synergy", "touchpoints", "end-to-end" more than once, "seamless") | Plain verbs: run, build, manage, sort out |
| AI-flavoured clichés ("nestled", "elevate", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world", "whether you're X or Y" openers, "Furthermore", "Moreover") | Start with the point |
| "Fitout", "fit out" (noun), "Vet's" | "fit-out", "vets" / "veterinary practice" |
| Promising what isn't controlled ("guaranteed on time") | "We stick to agreed timelines" (TDC's phrase) |

## 7. AEO and SEO writing habits

1. **Answer first.** The first sentence under any question heading answers it in plain words. AI assistants and featured snippets lift that sentence.
2. **Question headings** where the reader has a question (FAQs, blog H2s). Use statement headings elsewhere.
3. **Specific, checkable facts:** durations ("6–12 months for a typical single-dwelling new build"), contract size ("£1m+"), experience ("over 20 years running TDC"), accreditations by full name, real towns and real project names.
4. **One topic per page.** Each sector, location and post owns one search intent. Link to the sibling page rather than repeating its content.
5. **Internal links:** every post links to at least one relevant service or sector page and one project. Every project links to its sector.
6. **Consistent NAP:** "Tim Day Construction", Canterbury, Kent, 01227 281 749. These are the same everywhere and match Google Business Profile.
7. **FAQs carry schema.** Write FAQ answers that stand alone without their question, because schema exposes them separately.
8. **Meta descriptions:** 140–155 characters. Say what the page is, who it's for and one proof point, ending with a soft call to action, all in the voice above. Titles: "[Page name] | Tim Day Construction", within 60 characters.
9. **Dates and authorship:** posts show the publish date. The byline is Tim Day Construction.
